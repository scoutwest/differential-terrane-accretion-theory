C. WHAT FRICTION ACTUALLY ALLOWS (lab values; sources in DTAT Section 8.6)
   lizardite, water-saturated gouge, room T: mu 0.15-0.35. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9973; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9995 (at the stronger value).
   end-member lizardite / fibrous serpentine, to 170 C (Tesei et al. 2018): mu 0.15-0.19. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9951; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9990 (at the stronger value).
   antigorite, room T (Reinen): mu 0.5-0.85. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9989; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9998 (at the stronger value).
   antigorite + quartz at 500 C (2024 study): mu 0.22-0.22. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9958; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9992 (at the stronger value).
   serpentinite bare surface at seismic slip rate (Proctor et al. 2014): mu 0.1-0.1. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9907; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9981 (at the stronger value).
   serpentinite gouge at 1 m/s, 4-22 MPa (Proctor et al. 2014): mu 0.39-0.51. For tau <= 1 MPa need lambda >= 0.9982; for tau <= 0.2 MPa need >= 0.9996 (at the stronger value).
   Stress at hydrostatic-like pore pressure (lambda 0.4) with the weakest quoted mu (0.1): 64 MPa; with mu 0.2: 128 MPa.
   Lab normal stresses in these tests are 4-120 MPa, against about 1,070 MPa here; slip distances are metres, against 900-3,800 km.

D. READING
   1. To stay under the heating cap (about 0.04-1.0 MPa for a 40 mW/m2 craton, 0.16-3.7 MPa for a cool 28 mW/m2 craton),
      the slip surface needs lambda of about 0.997-0.9998 at mu 0.1-0.3. That is pore fluid held within 0.02-0.3% of the rock load.
   2. Friction alone, with any lab mu from 0.1 to 0.85, gives tens to hundreds of MPa at lambda 0.9 and still about 1-10 MPa at 0.99.
      So the slip layer works only if it is almost fully fluid-supported.
   3. Fluid at the rock load is what a dehydrating layer can produce, and shear heating drives dehydration, so a self-lubricating
      feedback is physically conceivable. It has not been modelled: it needs fluid trapped for 150 days (very low permeability),
      continuous supply from the reaction, and the layer must not drain by hydrofracture first.
   4. At 0.3 m/s the slip rate is in the range where laboratory friction weakens further (flash heating, melt, thermal pressurisation),
      but those tests are at 1-2 orders of magnitude lower normal stress and metres of slip.
   5. Lizardite, the weakest serpentine in the tests, is the low-temperature phase (it gives way to antigorite above roughly 300 C, from memory,
      not checked), so the weak values may not apply at craton Moho temperatures of 360-590 C, where antigorite (mu 0.5-0.85 at room T, about 0.2 at 500 C in a mixture) is expected.
   6. The antigorite limit leaves little room: 3-60 K for Moho temperatures of 535-592 C. Cooler cratons (28-32 mW/m2) leave 170-230 K
      but are far from the limit, which CMCT Section 25 already found.

LIMITS
   Friction values are from a single search pass of abstracts and summaries, not read from the papers. Hydrostatic-like lambda 0.4 is an
   assumption. Uniform shear in a zone of 1 or 3 km; no conduction out of the zone (a few km in 150 days would be ~3.6 m, so it is negligible);
   no latent heat; heating all in the zone. The melting limit of the crust base was not sourced; wet crust solidus is believed to be above
   the antigorite limit, so the dehydration limit is used as the tighter one (from memory). Normal stress is lithostatic with a constant density.
